# Frontiers — Distilled Findings

Paper references point to `papers.md` in this folder.

---

## F1 — World models: plan in imagination, act in reality

**Claim:** The strongest known path to sample-efficient agents is learning a predictive world model and planning inside it — collecting real experience only to correct the dream.

**Evidence:** World Models (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018) — behavior evolved entirely in hallucination; PlaNet/Dreamer (Hafner et al. 2019/2020) — value backpropagated through imagined trajectories; DreamerV3 (2023) — first Minecraft diamond from scratch, 150+ domains, fixed hyperparameters; MuZero (domain 02) — the game-tree instance.

**Why it matters:** LLMs predict tokens; agents need to predict CONSEQUENCES. World-model architectures are how you get consequence-prediction, and they are the substrate into which reasoning-style test-time search (domain 03, F5) naturally generalizes.

---

## F2 — Active inference: one objective for perception, action, and curiosity

**Claim:** Expected free energy decomposes into goal-seeking (pragmatic value) and information-seeking (epistemic value) — agents that act to minimize it are curious AND purposeful with no bolted-on reward shaping.

**Evidence:** Friston's active inference framework (2012, cross-ref domain 04); Tschantz et al. (2020) scaled it with deep nets, matching model-based RL and solving tasks that reward-only RL cannot; JEPA's planning-by-minimizing-latent-error (LeCun 2022) is an engineering cousin.

**Why it matters:** Reward design is the hardest part of RL systems (reward hacking, domain 03 F3). Active inference replaces hand-crafted curiosity bonuses with a term that falls out of the math — a principled alternative worth piloting wherever you currently write reward functions.

---

## F3 — Attention is associative memory: the energy-based lineage returns

**Claim:** Transformer attention is exactly one step of modern Hopfield retrieval — the LLM era secretly runs on 1982 physics, which re-opens the door to energy-based, memory-centric successors.

**Evidence:** Ramsauer et al. (2020) — the equivalence theorem; Hopfield (1982, domain 01) — content-addressable memory as energy minima; Titans (2025) and TTT layers (2024) as memory-first modern architectures.

**Why it matters:** If you're designing "beyond transformer" systems, the attention-as-memory frame tells you WHAT attention is FOR (storage + retrieval), so you can replace it deliberately (e.g., with learned write/forget policies) rather than by kernel substitution.

---

## F4 — The post-transformer zoo: each candidate encodes a different bias

**Claim:** There is no single "transformer killer"; there is a portfolio of architectures, each trading a different computational property.

**Evidence:** Mamba/Gu & Dao (2023): input-dependent selectivity, linear time, constant-memory streaming; RWKV (2023): parallel training + recurrent inference; Hyena (2023): subquadratic convolutions; xLSTM (2024): modernized gated recurrence; KAN (2024): learnable edge activations → symbolic compactness; TTT (2024): hidden states that learn during inference; Titans (2025): learned test-time memorization.

**Why it matters:** Match architecture to constraint: long-stream/edge → Mamba-family; symbolic/scientific discovery → KAN; adaptive-at-inference → TTT/Titans. Production trend (2025-26) is hybrids — attention layers for retrieval precision spliced into SSM layers for cheap context.

---

## F5 — Diffusion language models break the serial-generation constraint

**Claim:** Text can be generated by iterative parallel denoising rather than left-to-right token chaining — with competitive quality and radical throughput gains.

**Evidence:** LLaDA (2025) — 8B diffusion LM rivaling autoregressive LLaMA3-8B; Mercury (Inception Labs, 2025) — commercial diffusion LLM at 1000+ tokens/sec; Gemini Diffusion (2025) — DeepMind's research preview.

**Why it matters:** Generation speed becomes a hardware-parallel axis; bidirectional context and native infilling/editing come free. If autoregressive economics bind you (latency, cost), diffusion LMs are now a real alternative — the first crack in the AR orthodoxy since 2017.

---

## F6 — Biological substrates: intelligence per watt, not per FLOP

**Claim:** Spiking, liquid-time, and reservoir substrates target the class of intelligence that must run always-on, on milliwatts — a different optimization target than datacenter LLMs.

**Evidence:** Maass (1997) — spiking neurons computationally more powerful than sigmoid units; Loihi (2018) + Hala Point (2024) — neuromorphic hardware at 3-5 orders of magnitude lower energy; Liquid time-constant networks (2021) — deployed continuous-time control; Echo-state networks (2001) — dynamics as free feature maps; SpikeGPT (2023) — LLM architecture translated to spikes.

**Why it matters:** The intelligence market is splitting: cloud-scale reasoning vs. edge/embodied sensing. For the second class, train small and deploy on event-driven hardware; the reservoir/liquid lesson (random structure + tiny trained readout) remains the cheapest usable pattern.

---

## F7 — Neural-symbolic hybrids own systematic compositionality

**Claim:** Compositional, rule-following generalization comes most reliably from executing symbolic programs over neural perception — not from scale alone.

**Evidence:** DeepProbLog (2018) — gradients through probabilistic logic; Neuro-Symbolic Concept Learner (2019) — program execution answering VQA with human-like data efficiency; the failure side: Faith and Fate (Dziri et al. 2023, domain 03) — transformers' native multi-step circuits compound error exponentially; 2024-25 ARC solvers leaned neuro-symbolic.

**Why it matters:** For domains with verifiable structure (law, medicine, engineering, math), wrap LLM perception with explicit program/logic layers instead of hoping scale induces rigor. This is also the pragmatic architecture for ARC-class benchmarks.

---

## F8 — Open-endedness: objectives deceive; novelty discovers

**Claim:** Searching ONLY for the objective actively misleads when stepping stones to it look like failure — novelty-driven, objective-free search finds what objective search cannot.

**Evidence:** Lehman & Stanley (2011) — novelty search solving deceptive mazes; POET (2019) — co-evolving environments and solvers as an automatic curriculum; Evolution Strategies at scale (2017) — gradient-free parallel alternative.

**Why it matters:** For self-improving agents (F11) the environment generator is as important as the learner: co-evolve tasks with solvers, keep a population (diversity), and don't over-anchor on a single metric — or the system will Goodhart it (domain 03, F3).

---

## F9 — Collective intelligence: from emergent protocols to agent societies

**Claim:** Multiple communicating agents develop shared symbolic protocols under coordination pressure, and LLM-based populations now support society-scale simulation — collective behavior is a designed artifact.

**Evidence:** Emergent communication in referential games (Lazaridou et al. 2016-18) — language arises, but drifts alien without grounding biases; Generative Agents (Park et al. 2023) — 25 agents with memory/reflection produce believable social dynamics; MCP (2024) + agent-to-agent protocols (2025) — standard interfaces make heterogeneous agents composable.

**Why it matters:** The unit of intelligence is shifting from model to SYSTEM. Your leverage points are the protocol (what agents can say), the topology (who talks to whom — small-world, domain 04 F9), and the verification gate (who checks claims — F12 below).

---

## F10 — The 2026 agent stack is a settled pattern

**Claim:** Practical LLM agents converged on a repeating architecture: planner core + layered memory (episodic store, RAG index, skill library) + tool protocols + reflection loop + verification gate.

**Evidence:** Memory → Generative Agents (2023) memory streams; tools → Toolformer (2023) → MCP (2024); reflection → Reflexion (2023); skills → Voyager (2023) verified code library; verification → unit tests/verifiers in RLVR practice (domain 03, F4); see diagrams/agent-stack.mmd.md.

**Why it matters:** Don't invent agent architecture from scratch — instantiate this stack and spend your novelty budget on the memory format and the verifier quality, which is where systems actually differentiate. Every component descends from a specific paper; cite them when you build.

---

## F11 — Self-improvement loops are real — and are the sharpest double-edged object in the field

**Claim:** Systems that generate their own training signal, verify it, and re-train now demonstrably improve (STaR → R1-Zero → Absolute Zero) — closing the loop fully removes the human-data ceiling and the human oversight floor.

**Evidence:** STaR (2022) — rationale bootstrapping; DeepSeek-R1-Zero (2025) — pure RL eliciting reasoning with no SFT; Absolute Zero (2025) — one model proposes AND solves problems, zero external data, verified by code execution.

**Why it matters:** This is the direction of travel for frontier labs. Two engineering duties follow: (1) verifier integrity is now the whole safety surface — who checks the checker; (2) watch for model collapse / distribution narrowing (domain 01, F8's data-quality lessons apply recursively).

---

## F12 — Scalable oversight is a measurable engineering problem now

**Claim:** Supervising systems smarter than the supervisor has working mechanism candidates (debate, sandwiching, weak-to-strong elicitation) and a benchmark discipline — it is no longer pure philosophy.

**Evidence:** AI Safety via Debate (2018); scalable-oversight evaluation suite (Bowman et al. 2022) — sandwiching weak/strong models; Weak-to-Strong Generalization (2023) — strong models supervised by weak ones retain most capability (with known brittleness).

**Why it matters:** Any self-improving or agent system you deploy needs an oversight design: an adversarial check layer (debate-style), graded evaluators, and monitoring for the failure mode where the system learns what the checker rewards, not what you meant (Goodhart — domains 01 F13, 03 F3).

---

## F13 — Generality is measurable — and still unsolved: the ARC line

**Claim:** Skill-acquisition efficiency on novel problems (Chollet's definition, domain 01) is a measurable property, and frontier reasoning systems improve it only by burning enormous per-task compute — generality and efficiency remain unjointed.

**Evidence:** o3 on ARC-AGI-1 (2024): 75.7% (87.5% at 172× compute) — first big jump, at radical cost; ARC-AGI-2 (2025): humans solve most tasks; frontier models score single-to-low-double digits at release; Illusion-of-Thinking limits (domain 03, F6) reinforce the pattern.

**Why it matters:** Choose your target explicitly: skill (train bigger, distill) vs. generality (sample efficiency, program induction, priors). Benchmarks that separate them (ARC-class, procedurally generated) are the only trustworthy compass — LLM leaderboard wins increasingly measure skill, not intelligence.
